\subsection{Recruitment and Data Filtering}
In total, 28 raw survey responses were recorded. We excluded 4 participants identified as outliers based on predefined criteria. After removing the pilot session and 4 outliers, our final dataset consisted of $N = 23$ valid participants.

Due to the fully within-subjects design with 3 notification systems (Earcon, Speech, SonoAdapt), 2 urgency/importance levels (low, high), and 2 scenarios (interview, math), each participant completed 12 trials, yielding a total of 276 individual observations (23 participants $\times$ 12 trials) for the statistical analysis.

The final sample consisted of 23 participants (14 female, 9 male). Participants were between 21 and 40 years old ($M = 28.83$, $SD = 5.45$). As the study was conducted in English, participants self-reported their English proficiency. All participants indicated at least a good level of proficiency, with 20 (87.0\%) reporting that they understood English very well or perfectly. Regarding smart glasses experience, 22 participants (95.7\%) reported having no prior experience with smart glasses.

\subsection{Background}
As in the formative study (see Section~\ref{sec:formative_background}), we collected background information on participants' prior smart glasses experience, smartphone notification habits, and attitudes toward auditory notifications on smart glasses.

Similarly to the formative study, the vast majority of participants had no prior experience with smart glasses: 22 (95.7\%) had never used smart glasses, while one participant (4.3\%) had tried them once or twice.

Regarding which notifications participants would want to receive auditorily through smart glasses, phone calls remained the most frequently selected type (78.3\%), followed by navigation instructions (73.9\%), calendar or reminder alerts (69.6\%), and direct instant messages such as WhatsApp (52.2\%). Emails were selected less frequently (26.1\%). These preferences closely mirror those observed in the formative study.

Participants also showed a clear preference for reserving auditory notifications for messages of higher relevance. Overall, 78.3\% agreed (somewhat, or strongly) that they would only want to be notified auditorily about important messages, while this proportion increased to 87.0\% for urgent messages.

Consistent with the formative study, the active use of audio notifications on participants' smartphones was uncommon, with only 13.0\% enabling audible alerts. Most participants relied on visual notifications combined with vibration (47.8\%), visual notifications only (21.7\%), or had notifications disabled entirely (17.4\%). The most frequently cited reasons for these settings were social etiquette (60.9\%) and personal focus (56.5\%), again aligning with the formative findings.
